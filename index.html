About

The story behind the code

I spent five years inside large tech companies — Bosch, Infineon, Ericsson — solving people-and-process problems in HR. Somewhere along the way I realised I kept wanting to build the tools myself instead of waiting for someone else to.

So I started. I taught myself Python, built automation tools on my own initiative, and evolved them into Universal HR HUB — a modular platform I use daily at Ericsson, built around human-in-the-loop steps before documents are exported or sent to print. I got asked to extend the concept for teams globally, all without a developer job title. I also work with DICE, Ericsson's internal Native AI dashboard team, as process advisor and unofficial Scrum Master — facilitating sprints with mentor support while shadowing another team through a full delivery workflow.

I use AI daily to speed up design, prototyping, and documentation — but I still own architecture, validation, and release decisions. My automation follows a clear pattern: calculate, review, generate, approve, then ship. I'm growing toward workflow architecture while still building the first version myself.

My edge is context. I don't just write code — I understand why the problem exists, who it affects, and what "good enough" actually means inside a large organisation. I'm targeting a junior developer or QA role where that HR and process depth is an asset, not a footnote.
