My job title has said Talent Acquisition for a long time. My work has always been wider than that.

Budapest · 2019–2021

Uptime under pressure

Before HR, I kept machines alive. At a VR park I learned what “uptime” feels like when people are waiting — diagnostics under pressure, documentation so the next person isn’t lost, systems that have to work for twelve hours straight. That instinct never left.

Bosch · 2022–2024

Learning the grammar of HR

Then I stepped into people operations. As an HRBP intern I drafted contracts, tended SAP records, prepared materials for tough conversations about pay and exits. As a recruiter I screened at volume, called candidates, moved offers, and sat at career fairs representing a brand larger than myself.

I learned the grammar of enterprise HR: compliance, calendars, kindness under load. I also learned how much time disappears into copy-paste and waiting.

Infineon · 2024–2025

Pipelines and trust

Emerging talent and university relations stretched the map. Dual education logistics. Assessment centers with thirty to fifty people in a room. Partnerships with BME, Óbuda, technical schools. Open days on a factory floor. Reporting to a central team in Porto while Hungary stayed local and real.

That year was about pipelines and trust — academic partners, Gen-Z candidates who expect speed, and the quiet work of making a company feel reachable.

Ericsson · 2025 – Present

Own the lifecycle — shape the process

Now I own the Hungarian talent acquisition lifecycle end to end — intake with hiring managers, screening, interviews, offers, negotiations, contracts, pre-boarding, mobility — under global standards, with audit trails that have to hold.

In parallel I facilitate Scrum for a student team developing an internal AI agent: stand-ups, planning, demos, retrospectives, mentors above me, a hundred percent of sprint goals delivered. Process stopped being something I only administered. It became something I could shape.

The turn

Build the missing piece

Somewhere between the tickets and the templates I stopped waiting for IT to invent the missing piece. I taught myself Python. I built automation for the workflows I lived in — vacation logic, contract tokens, print queues — with a human still in the loop before anything ships.

That is the through-line. I understand why the problem exists, who it hurts when it’s slow, and what “good enough” means inside a matrix. Then I build toward that.

Alongside · Ötpróba

Stamina is also a skill

For more than four years, on weekends and peak seasons, I have run event operations for a national sports organization — registration flows, contingencies, brand on the ground — while holding full-time HR roles.
